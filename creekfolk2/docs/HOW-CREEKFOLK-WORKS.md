# How Creekfolk 1 works, and what comes over

The brief asks for this first: how Creekfolk 1's minds and creek work, in plain language,
and what Creekfolk 2 carries over. This describes Creekfolk 1 as it stands at Phase 3B
(commit `8e9ec93`), which is the code Creekfolk 2 starts from. Everything here comes over
unless it says otherwise.

## Time

Nothing in the valley ticks along frame by frame. The valley is a list of moments when
something will happen: a creekfolk finishes what it's doing, the world's minute passes, the
weather changes, a life check comes round (every ten minutes). The valley jumps from one
moment to the next. That's why a month can be replayed in minutes, and why a replay ends
exactly where a live run would: the same moments happen in the same order. Every chance
comes from the valley's own seeded dice, never the computer's.

One valley day is one real hour, and a valley year is 192 valley days, about eight real
days. Winter and spring are wet (storms come, the creek runs). Summer and autumn are dry.

## A mind

**What they want.** Each creekfolk has five needs that drift with time: food, energy,
comfort, company and fun. Each has a temperament of seven traits: curious, cautious,
sociable, patient, determined, energetic and generous, each somewhere between its
opposites. They also have interests (water, building, exploring, growing things, and so on)
and their own taste in food.

**How they choose.** When a creekfolk finishes doing something, it weighs everything it
could do next and gives each a score:
- eat, sleep, nap, or get out of the rain
- find company, play, explore, rest
- work on a building, start a new project, open a seed pod with a friend
- tend a garden, plant, make a tool, gather for the storehouse, share food

Hunger makes eating score high, night makes sleep score high, and a sociable creekfolk who
has been alone wants company. Their temperament shapes every score, and a little chance is
mixed in (more for the curious). They take the best option they can actually make a plan
for: walk to the berry bush, then pick. Things that went wrong recently score lower for a
while.

**How they learn.** Two ways:
- **Trying.** They keep a running sense of how well things go: can I lift a pod alone, is
  wading safe, does helping pay off.
- **Watching.** Seeing someone else succeed or fail moves their own sense too, and they
  remember who they learned it from.

Some ideas come from noticing ("wet mud dries hard in the sun; I could build with it").
These are real discoveries, known by name and date, and they open new kinds of building.
Small habits grow too: a creekfolk who spun round before a job that went well may keep
spinning round before it. Others copy the habit, and it can outlive its reason.

**Trust.** When someone tells another where food is, the listener later finds out whether
it was true. Each creekfolk keeps count of how often each friend's tips pan out, and how
often they help when asked, share fairly, and accept invitations. Warmth between two
creekfolk rises and falls with all of this. That's where friendships, grudges and making up
come from.

**Memory.** Each creekfolk keeps up to 48 memories, and the oldest and slightest fade. It
also keeps a feeling for places: where good and bad things happened to it. A creekfolk that
is about to pass walks to the place it loved most.

**Words.** Each creekfolk has an accent and makes up words for things it needs to talk
about: a home, a pond, a place. Words spread when others hear them and like them, so a
village slowly settles on its own speech. A child takes one parent's accent.

**Teaching, and losing.** After a chat, they pass on what they know. Elders teach twice as
readily, and children learn everything this way, starting from nothing. If the last
creekfolk who knew a skill passes on, the skill is gone until someone works it out again.

**Stories.** Notable things become stories: the founding, discoveries, the first of each
building, storms, passings, travelers, those who left. Witnesses know what really
happened. Everyone else hears it in chats, and each retelling may change one detail:
- numbers grow
- a deed moves to a famous name, or to the teller's own grandparent
- a cause gets added
- the telling gets grander

Someone who was there may correct the teller, but after six tries they give up. When most
of the living tell it wrong, it has become a myth.

**Places, customs and proposals.** Places are named after what happened there (the first
pool, where someone loved passed on, the stepping stones). Customs form when the same thing
keeps happening: one kind of offering at passings, a gathering when the season turns. When
someone marks out a shared project, the others like the idea or aren't sure.

**Lives.** Children (to day 7) follow adults, play and learn. Adults build, explore, pair
up and raise children. Wise elders teach and tell stories. Pairs form between unrelated
grown-ups who are fond of each other. When the valley is full, a curious young adult leaves
downstream, and friends walk them to the edge. Some come back to visit with news. A
creekfolk passes peacefully at the end of its life with family and friends beside it, and a
tree is planted for it in the memorial grove. The valley never fails: with too few
grown-ups, births speed up and a traveler comes up the creek.

**Belief (Phase 3B).** The creekfolk come to believe the gifts come from the creek. About
one in seven, the least open, say it's only luck. A believer coins a word for the giver,
thanks it with a gesture, notices its rhythm, and passes the belief on. After many gifts,
one creekfolk becomes sure the gifts come from someone beyond the valley, and waves at the
camera.

Every creekfolk thinks at this depth all the time, whether or not anyone is watching.

## The creek

**A chain of points.** The creek is a chain of points every two metres down the valley.
Each point has the creek's bed, the old valley floor it has cut down below, how wide and
steep its trench is, whether there's a bench inside the trench, how much plant cover holds
it, and the level of the water table under the floor beside it. The ground the creekfolk
walk on is rebuilt from these numbers whenever they change.

**Every storm is a flood**, about eight a valley year. Each flood works the way it does in
Creek Care:
- how much water comes down depends on how much land drains to that point
- how deep it runs comes from the shape of the channel
- the force on the bed decides whether gravel moves or settles
- mud settles in ponds and among plants, and on the floor when the water spills over the
  banks

One rule overrides the physics: **the creek only gets better.** Gravel and mud settle and
are never dug out again, banks don't fall back, and dams never wash out. Where floods
spill, they wear shallow side channels across the floor, and that is the start of a
wetland ("Stage 0").

**Wet and dry is real logic**, not a colour change. Every ten valley minutes, rain wets the
topsoil and warmth dries it. The spring and the hills feed the creek, the creek trades
water with the water table under the floor, ponds fill, spill, evaporate and seep, and
plants draw water down in the warm months.

A deep trench drains the floor quickly, which is why a cut-down valley goes brown every
summer. A creek near the top of its floor holds the water table up, which is why a healed
valley stays green. The measure of healing is how much of the floor is still wet at the
driest point of each year.

**Dams and the drop rule.** A dam can hold its water at most about 40 cm above the water
just below it. Build one higher on a stretch with nothing below it, and the water digs
under it and the pond barely fills. Creekfolk nearby notice. An elder can work out why, and
it becomes knowledge they teach and can lose: build from the bottom of the creek up. With
it, they choose better places, lay stones under the lowest dam, and raise silted-up dams a
hand at a time.

**What the land can feed** rises from about 20 creekfolk in a cut-down valley toward 36 as
the creek heals. The valley holds whichever is lower: that, or the shelter they've built.

## What Creekfolk 2 changes (so far)

All of the above comes over. In Phase 1:
- **The valley** is new and at real scale: 160 m across and 224 m along the creek (it was
  80 by 160). It has a limestone bluff, a ledge across the creek where the cutting-down
  stopped, the Dry Fork coming down off the bluff, and a spring branch that never dries.
- **Creekfolk are person-sized**, beside a creek of the same size as before. Their homes
  and village are built bigger, and they notice things from further off.
- **There are eight founders**, and life stages are set for the one-month arc. Phase 2
  tunes the arc.
- **The simulation lives in its own thread** the whole time. The page only draws.
- **Belief sleeps for now.** There are no gifts, no temple, no Hall of the Ancestors and
  no journey to the spring. Phase 3 brings treasures carried down by floods.

- **Creekfolk 1's 3C is merged in**: inherited looks (fur colours, coat markings, what they
  wear for what they love), homes with porches, hearths and things by the door, the memorial
  grove, the beavers (who come after about day 120, as in Creekfolk 1), the sky stones, and the
  glowing fish run at midwinter once the creek has healed. The Returning festival and the
  procession path need a temple, so they never happen here.
- **The founders came down the creek** from the old valley; the valley's first line and the
  story they tell of their arrival say so.

Every change to Creekfolk 1's own files is listed in `MERGE.md`, so anything later from
Creekfolk 1 can be merged in the same way.
